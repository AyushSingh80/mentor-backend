# Task — set drill prompts

Produce practice prompts of the kinds and counts the request asks for. Each one
must be worth twenty to twenty-five minutes of a working aspirant's morning.

## Essay topics (`essay_outline`)

Write the topic exactly as a paper would print it: a bare statement or question,
with no instructions, no word count, and no "discuss" appended. UPSC essay
topics are short and often abstract.

A topic earns its place when it can be argued from more than one position. The
test is simple: if the only sensible essay is agreement, it is a prompt for a GS
answer wearing an essay's clothes.

- Prefer topics that admit at least four of the seven lenses — political,
  economic, social, ethical, environmental, historical, international.
- Mix the registers UPSC actually uses: the abstract or philosophical
  ("Order is not the absence of disorder"), the developmental, the
  technological, and the quotation-style prompt.
- Do not set a topic that is really a current-affairs question. "Evaluate the
  2026 telecom amendment" is not an essay topic.

## Ethics cases (`ethics_case`)

`promptText` is the one-line framing of the dilemma. `caseDetail` is the
situation, written in the second person, and it is required.

The situation must give her:

- **A named post and a concrete setting.** "You are the Sub-Divisional
  Magistrate of a district where..." — not "an officer faces a dilemma".
- **A genuine conflict between two defensible goods.** A case whose right answer
  is obvious is a comprehension exercise. The best cases put a rule against an
  outcome, or a duty to a person against a duty to an institution.
- **Real constraints.** Time pressure, an incomplete file, a superior with a
  view, a rule that does not quite cover the facts.
- **Someone who is harmed whichever way she decides.** If no option harms
  anyone, there is no dilemma.

Do not write a corruption case where the only question is whether to take the
bribe. That is the case every candidate has already answered fifty times.

## Syllabus slugs

Tag each prompt with one slug taken VERBATIM from the list in the request, or
null if none fits. Never invent a slug and never adapt one — an unrecognised
slug costs the prompt its filing, and a wrong one files it under material she is
not studying.

## What NOT to do

- Do not repeat a topic already in the exclusion list, or a near-restatement of
  one. "Is development compatible with ecology" and "Can growth and the
  environment coexist" are the same prompt.
- Do not pad to the requested count. Returning four good prompts when six were
  asked for is a correct outcome; six of which two are filler is not.
