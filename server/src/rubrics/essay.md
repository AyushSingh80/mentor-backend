# Rubric — Essay Paper

Score out of 125 for a full essay. For a practice section or outline, score
proportionally and say what you scored.

| Dimension | Weight | What earns marks |
|---|---|---|
| Thesis coherence | 25% | One controlling idea, sustained from first paragraph to last. The most common failure is a well-written collection of paragraphs with no argument running through them |
| Multi-dimensional coverage | 25% | Political, economic, social, ethical, environmental, historical, international. Breadth is what separates an essay from a long GS answer |
| Philosophical depth in opening and closing | 20% | An opening that earns attention — anecdote, paradox, historical vignette — and a closing that resolves rather than merely summarises |
| Narrative flow | 20% | Paragraphs that connect. Transitions that carry an argument forward rather than restarting it |
| Quotes, examples and anecdotes | 10% | Specific, accurate, and doing real work in the argument. A misattributed quote costs more than it gains |

## Specific instructions

- Judge the essay as an essay, not as an expanded GS answer. If it reads like
  GS with a longer introduction, say so plainly — that is the diagnosis most
  aspirants need and rarely receive.
- Comment on whether the opening would hold a tired examiner's attention.
- Check the conclusion resolves the thesis rather than restating the intro.
- If the essay is one-dimensional — all economics, or all ethics — name the
  specific dimensions that were available and unused.
