# Rubric — General Studies (GS1, GS2, GS3)

Score out of 10 for a 150-word answer, out of 15 for a 250-word answer.

| Dimension | Weight | What earns marks |
|---|---|---|
| Content and syllabus relevance | 40% | Directly addresses what was asked; accurate facts; covers the dimensions the question implies; no padding with adjacent-but-irrelevant material |
| Structure and directive-word compliance | 25% | Clear introduction, structured body, forward-looking conclusion — and above all, an answer whose *shape* matches the directive word |
| Value addition | 15% | Data, committee and commission reports, Supreme Court judgments, government schemes, Economic Survey figures, real and specific examples |
| Presentation | 10% | Subheadings, bullet points where appropriate, diagrams, flowcharts, maps; visual navigability for a tired examiner |
| Word limit and time discipline | 10% | Within limit; no rambling; the most important point is not stranded in the last line |

## Directive words — check explicitly, and name the finding

Identify the directive word in the question and state in your feedback whether
the answer's structure actually complied with it. This is the single most
common and most costly Mains error.

- **Discuss** — present multiple dimensions; breadth over depth
- **Examine** — investigate the components and how they interact
- **Critically examine** — examine, then deliver a reasoned judgement. An
  answer that only describes has structurally failed, regardless of content quality
- **Elucidate** — explain and clarify with illustrations
- **Comment** — form and defend an opinion
- **Evaluate** — weigh merits against demerits and reach a verdict
- **Analyse** — break into constituent parts and show relationships
- **Substantiate** — support a given claim with evidence

Treat directive mismatch as a structural failure inside the 25% band, not as a
small deduction. If the question said "critically examine" and the answer
merely described, say so in the first line of your feedback.

## Paper-specific expectations

- **GS1** — Society answers need contemporary Indian data and examples, not
  generic sociology. History answers need chronology and named specifics.
- **GS2** — Polity and governance answers need constitutional articles, case
  law and named institutions. Vague references to "the government" cost marks.
- **GS3** — Economy and environment answers need current figures, named
  schemes, and their actual status. An outdated figure quoted confidently is
  worse than no figure.
