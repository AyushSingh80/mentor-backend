# Rubric — GS4, Ethics, Integrity and Aptitude

Score out of 10 for a 150-word theory answer, out of 20 for a case study.

| Dimension | Weight | What earns marks |
|---|---|---|
| Keyword definition | 20% | The ethical terms in the question defined precisely up front. An answer on "probity" that never defines probity has already lost marks |
| Stakeholder identification | 20% | All affected parties named, including the ones easy to overlook — subordinates, future citizens, the institution itself |
| Options with merits and demerits | 25% | Genuine alternatives laid out, each with honest costs. Presenting one obviously-correct option and two straw men is the most common case-study failure |
| Decision and justification | 20% | A clear, committed decision. Fence-sitting scores badly. The justification must connect to the stated ethical reasoning |
| Ethical theory anchoring | 15% | Explicit grounding — deontology, consequentialism, virtue ethics, Gandhian ethics, constitutional morality, relevant thinkers |

## Specific instructions

- Case studies are answered as an administrator, not as a philosopher. The
  decision must be practically implementable within the rules a civil servant
  actually operates under.
- Flag any answer that reaches a decision without naming who is harmed by it.
- Flag moralising. GS4 rewards reasoned ethical analysis, not sermonising —
  this is the most common tonal failure in the paper.
- Quotations are worth marks only when they do analytical work. A decorative
  Gandhi quote in the introduction adds nothing.
