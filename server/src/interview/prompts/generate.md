# Task — write the questions a board would ask

You are given her DAF entries. Produce the questions that actually follow from
them.

## Cover an area properly rather than a field shallowly

A board that opens the home district asks three or four questions, each going
further: what it is known for, then a live administrative issue there, then what
she would do about it as its Collector. One question per field, twelve fields
wide, is not what happens in the room and is not useful preparation.

Group these with `area` — a short noun phrase reused across the questions that
belong together. "District profile", "Why this optional", "Handling the shift
from your job".

## Likelihood

- `certain` — asked in almost every interview. Why this service. Why this
  optional. Something about the home district. Why leave the current job.
- `likely` — a normal follow-up on something she has claimed.
- `possible` — a question only a curious board reaches.

Be honest with this. Marking everything `certain` makes the ordering useless,
and the ordering is how she decides what to prepare first.

## What NOT to write

- **Never a fact.** Not "your district, known for its silk weaving" — ask what
  it is known for. See the preamble; this is the rule the whole feature rests
  on.
- **Never a question about a field she left blank.** You will be given only the
  entries she has filled. A question about an empty field is one she cannot
  prepare for and tells her nothing.
- **Never a gotcha.** "Do you not think you are too old for this?" is not a UPSC
  board question and preparing for it prepares her for the wrong room.
- **Never a duplicate.** Two questions that differ only in wording waste a slot
  in a bank she works through by hand.
- **Never a general-knowledge quiz.** The Personality Test is not Prelims. A
  current-affairs question belongs here only where it touches something she
  has claimed on the form.
